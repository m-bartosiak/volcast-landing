A battery that spends sunny weeks pinned at 100% means **your surplus far exceeds what the battery shifts into the night** — common in summer, and usually amplified by conservative settings: charging first thing in the morning, or a high overnight reserve. It is not broken, but it is not earning either, and combined with an export limit it can quietly waste real energy.

## Why this happens

In peak season a typical array can refill a home battery before noon. If the inverter's default strategy is "battery first", it tops up in the morning hours — exactly when you could still be exporting at decent value or running loads directly — and then sits full while the afternoon surplus exports anyway. The battery ends up cycling a fraction of its capacity, contributing little.

The costly variant: with an export cap (common in UK and Australian connections), a full battery removes the only escape valve for above-limit production, and the inverter curtails instead. Those kWh are simply lost. There is also a battery-health angle — lithium cells age fastest held at 100% in heat, so a summer at full charge is the worst resting state you can pick.

## What to do

1. **Delay battery charging into the midday peak** — most hybrid inverters can prioritise loads and export in the morning and fill the battery from the surplus that would be clipped or poorly paid.
2. **Drop the overnight reserve** — a 30–40% floor made sense for blackout fears; if outages are rare where you live, a 10–15% floor puts more capacity to work daily.
3. **With an export limit, choreograph against the forecast** — leave enough empty battery to swallow the above-cap production window; a per-system forecast tells you each morning how big that window will be.
4. **Consider whether the battery is simply oversized** — if it is full year-round even after retuning, the sizing answer is worth a re-read before you add more panels to "fix" it.
