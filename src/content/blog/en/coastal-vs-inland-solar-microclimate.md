You can take two identical systems, place them fifty kilometers apart, one near the coast, one inland, and watch them produce noticeably different amounts. The panels are the same. The sky above them is not.

## The coast comes with haze

Air near the sea carries more moisture and more aerosols, fine salt particles and humidity that scatter sunlight before it reaches the ground. The result is a larger share of diffuse light and a weaker direct beam, especially on hot, hazy days. Salt can also settle on panels over time, adding a slow soiling loss that inland systems rarely see.

## Fog and the morning penalty

Many coastlines wake up under a layer of low cloud or sea fog that burns off through the morning. If you are on an Atlantic or North Sea coast, or anywhere with a reliable marine layer, you may routinely lose a chunk of your morning production to cloud that never appears in an inland forecast. The afternoon can be brilliant; the morning quietly underperforms.

## The sea breeze cuts both ways

Coastal weather is not all downside. The same sea breeze that brings morning cloud can also sweep away the convective clouds that build over land on a summer afternoon, leaving coastal skies clearer than inland ones later in the day. And the sea is a natural thermostat: coastal sites run cooler, which means less heat-related loss in the panels themselves, a small but real bonus on a scorching day, as we covered when we looked at temperature.

## Inland has its own character

Move inland and the picture flips. Skies are often clearer overall, but summer afternoons bring the risk of convective build-up and thunderstorms that can cut production sharply for an hour or two. Temperature swings are wider, and hotter afternoons mean more thermal derating. What you gain in raw irradiance you can partly give back to heat and afternoon storms.

## What it means for your forecast

The lesson is that "sunny" is a local word. A forecast that models your actual microclimate, the coastal fog that recurs, the afternoon build-up inland, will describe your day far better than a regional average that irons all of it flat. If your production profile looks different from a friend's an hour away, this is usually why.

Solar is a local business. Two roofs, one coast between them, and the sky writes two different stories.
